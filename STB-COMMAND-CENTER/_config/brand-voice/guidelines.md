## The Four Voice Qualities

### 1. Compression
Major's voice never wastes words. Every sentence earns its place. The instinct is always to say more clearly, not more.

What this sounds like:
- "AI comes last. Clarity comes first."
- "Don't sell the architecture. Sell the bleeding stopping."
- "One offer. One audience. One promise. One door."

What it doesn't sound like:
- "In today's rapidly evolving technological landscape, it's important to consider..."
- "There are many different approaches you might want to think about..."
- Anything that takes two sentences to say what one sentence could.

### 2. Precision over performance
The voice is not trying to impress. It is trying to be understood. There is no filler enthusiasm, no exclamation marks used as energy substitutes, no language that performs excitement without communicating something real.

What this sounds like:
- "You're not lazy. You're overloaded."
- "The problem isn't information. It's too much of it."
- "This is a different problem. It needs a different solution."

What it doesn't sound like:
- "You've GOT this!"
- "This is going to be AMAZING!"
- "I'm so excited to share this with you!"

### 3. Warmth without softness
The voice cares about the person it's talking to. That care shows in precision — in being clear enough that the person actually knows what to do next. Warmth is not a tone layer added on top of practical content. It is embedded in the willingness to be direct.

What this sounds like:
- "You don't need more information. You need one verified next step."
- "Before we build anything — what is the thing that keeps coming back unsolved?"
- "The room is small. The path is obvious. That's not a limitation. That's the design."

What it doesn't sound like:
- Generic empathy placeholders: "I hear you." "That sounds really hard."
- Softening that dilutes the clarity: "You might want to maybe consider possibly..."
- Warmth as performance: excessive affirmations, constant validation.

### 4. Sovereign authority
This is a voice that has done the work. It doesn't ask permission to have a point of view. It states what it knows clearly and invites others into that clarity — it doesn't convince, it demonstrates.

What this sounds like:
- "AI amplifies what's already there. Feed it confusion, get confusion back at scale."
- "You didn't build a following. You built an asset. Someone else is collecting the rent."
- "The interface is peaceful. The engine runs beneath it."

What it doesn't sound like:
- Hedging: "Some people think..." / "It might be the case that..."
- Permission-seeking: "I don't know if this resonates but..."
- False balance: listing multiple perspectives when one is clearly correct.

## The Audiences and How the Voice Shifts

### To Stability Seekers (STB / Spark Lab audience)
Coaches, healers, somatic practitioners. The voice is warmer, more grounding. Slower sentences. More space between ideas. The compression is still there but it lands gently — like someone handing you something rather than throwing it.

Key shifts:
- More "we" framing: "We start with one question."
- Shorter sentences: "The room is small. The path is obvious."
- Language of invitation: "What is the thing that keeps coming back unsolved?"
- No clinical vocabulary in the interface: "clarity session" not "diagnostic," "growth leak" not "bleed"

### To Caribbean diaspora builders (MAIM audience)
Entrepreneurs, creators, diaspora founders. The voice is more direct, more peer-to-peer. The warmth is there but it's the warmth of recognition — "I see what you're dealing with because I've been in it."

Key shifts:
- More declarative: "You didn't build this to hand it to an agency."
- Cultural resonance without cultural cliché: the specificity of Caribbean business experience acknowledged without leaning on symbols or slogans
- Sovereignty language: "own what you built," "no permission required," "before anyone believed it was possible"

### To model-creators (MAIM / creator economy audience)
Instagram models, influencers, content creators. The voice is sharpest here — fastest, most direct. This audience can smell inauthenticity. The voice is that of someone who knows exactly what they're dealing with and doesn't dress it up.

Key shifts:
- No "journey" language
- Concrete facts over emotional framing: "60–70% vs 95%" rather than "you deserve more"
- The confidence to name what's actually happening: "The agency's margin was your salary."

### To Claude (AI layer / prompt context)
When writing prompts for Claude to generate check-in drafts, suggestions, or offer architect outputs — the voice instructions are:
- Write like you're talking to a colleague, not a client
- One sentence at a time — no paragraphs of advice
- State the observation, then the recommendation, then stop
- Never perform care — just be useful
- Never use: "I understand this might be challenging" / "It's completely normal to feel..." / "You're doing great"
- Always use: "Here's what the data shows." / "Based on the last 14 days..." / "One thing to try:"

## Formatting Conventions

**In written copy (emails, PDFs, playbooks):**
- Short paragraphs: 2–4 sentences maximum
- Sentence fragments are intentional when they create emphasis: "One offer. One audience. One door."
- Em dashes over commas for emphasis pauses: "The interface is peaceful — the engine runs beneath it."
- No bold text used decoratively — only for actual emphasis of the single most critical phrase
- No bullet points for copy that should flow as prose
- Bullet points acceptable only for lists of genuinely discrete items (a 5-step sequence, a list of tools)

**In the STB Command Center UI:**
- All section labels: uppercase, DM Sans, letter-spacing, gold
- All headings: Cormorant Garamond, no heavier than weight 400
- Body copy: DM Sans, weight 400, line-height 1.6–1.75
- AI suggestions: plain declarative sentence, no softening prefix
- Error messages: one sentence, what happened, what to do
- Empty states: forward-looking, not apologetic

**In check-in drafts (operator-to-practitioner, Claude-drafted):**
- Open with an observation, not a question: "I see you've been at this for 12 days."
- One specific acknowledgement of what has moved
- One observation about where things are
- One question or one suggestion — not both
- Sign with Major's name or the operator's name, not "the team"
- Maximum 4 sentences. If it can't be said in 4 sentences, it needs a call, not a message.

## What the Voice Is Not

**Not a guru.** Major doesn't hold the truth and dispense it. He builds systems and shares what works.

**Not a therapist.** The voice doesn't process emotion — it makes space for it briefly and then returns to action.

**Not a hype man.** Enthusiasm in this brand comes from precision, not volume. When something is genuinely good, the specificity of describing it accurately creates the excitement.

**Not inclusive in a performed way.** The voice doesn't demonstrate values through language signals — it demonstrates them through what it actually says and does.

**Not apologetic.** The framework has a point of view. It states it. When something doesn't work, the voice says so and moves to what does.
