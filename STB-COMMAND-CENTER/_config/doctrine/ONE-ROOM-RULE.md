# One-Room Rule
**STB Core Doctrine · Layer 3 Reference**

---

## The Principle

The backend can be as complex as it needs to be. The client-facing interface must always be peaceful.

When a practitioner opens the STB Command Center, they should feel the same thing every time: *this is manageable. I know what to do.*

The room is small. The path is obvious.

---

## Applied to the Command Center

**Today screen:** Maximum 3 items visible above the fold before any scroll.
1. Momentum score card
2. One Focus
3. One Action + One Step

Nothing else. No notifications. No pending tasks. No upsells. One room.

**Evidence log:** The log button is the first interactive element after the header. One action. Two choices: did it / didn't.

**Sprint board:** Max 5 active tasks visible before "show more." Completed tasks are collapsed by default. The practitioner sees what needs doing, not what's done.

**Offer clarity:** One sentence at the top. One first doorway card. The second doorway is deliberately muted (60% opacity) — it exists but doesn't compete.

**AI suggestions:** One suggestion at a time. Never stack multiple Claude pills on the same screen. One prompt, one decision.

---

## What This Rule Prevents

- Task list anxiety (seeing 27 things when you open the app)
- Decision paralysis at the top of the day
- The practitioner feeling like the tool is demanding something from them
- The backend complexity bleeding into the front-end experience

---

## The Operator Exception

The operator view (Stage 06) is designed for the operator — someone who *wants* to see the whole cohort. The One-Room Rule doesn't apply there. It's a different room, for a different person, with different needs.

The doctrine is: the practitioner's room must stay calm. The operator's room can be complex.

---

## Copy Implications

- Greeting: one line. "Good morning, [name]." Nothing else.
- Section labels: short, directional. "ONE FOCUS" not "Tell us what you're working on today."
- Buttons: verb only. "Confirm" not "Confirm this suggestion and apply it to my focus."
- Error states: one sentence. What happened, what to do. No apology.

---

## Test Question

Before shipping any screen change, ask: *if a practitioner opened this at 7am, half-awake, would they know immediately what to do?*

If the answer is "maybe" — simplify until it's yes.
