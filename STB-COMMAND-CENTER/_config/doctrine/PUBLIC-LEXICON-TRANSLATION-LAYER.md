# The Public Lexicon: Translation Layer for Wellness Practitioners
**STB Canonical Doctrine · New File · From NotebookLM deep critique**
**Critical insight:** Clinical urgency language that converts B2B operators actively repels somatic practitioners.

---

## The Problem: The Framework Triggers the People It's Trying to Heal

The STB framework was built with operational rigor for maximum diagnostic precision. That rigor is the framework's greatest strength — and its greatest liability at the front door.

The NotebookLM critique identified a profound irony:

> "The framework is trying to help a demographic that specializes in regulating nervous systems, but it is inadvertently using language that triggers a sympathetic nervous system fight-or-flight response right at the digital doorway."

A somatic healer encountering "Find My Bleed" doesn't feel urgency. They feel alarm. Their entire professional worldview is built around nervous system regulation, trauma resolution, and holistic growth. Language that reads as direct and clinical to a tech CEO reads as medical crisis to a breathwork facilitator.

The Client Interface Doctrine mandates a peaceful interface. But a technically peaceful interface (one button, one action) is not emotionally peaceful if the words on the button are wrong.

**The fix:** A dedicated public-facing lexicon that wraps the same operational rigor in language that matches the natural resonance of the audience.

## The Translation Layer: Internal vs. External Vocabulary

### Primary Call to Action

| Internal Use | Public-Facing Language | Why It Works |
|-------------|----------------------|--------------|
| "Find My Bleed" | "Discover My Hidden Asset" | Frames the diagnostic as uncovering dormant value, not searching for injury |
| "Find My Bleed" | "Identify My Growth Leak" | Retains the operational concept (leak) but surrounds it with forward-looking context (growth) |
| "Find My Bleed" | "Illuminate My Next Step" | Positions the practitioner as an empowered visionary, not a patient in triage |
| "Find My Bleed" | "Uncover What's Holding You Back" | Gentle and directional — what's holding, not what's bleeding |

**Recommended primary CTA:** "Identify My Growth Leak"
**Reason:** Still operational (leak = systemic loss), still specific, but emotionally safe for a healer to click. Changes the user's posture from defensive to curious.

### The Internal/External Separation

**The doctrine is not to eliminate clinical language. It is to quarantine it.**

Internal use (operator, doctrine files, Claude AI layer, STB Command Center operator view):
→ Use the full clinical vocabulary. Precision matters. "Primary Bleed," "Intervention Router," "Machine-Readable Evidence Layer," "Diagnostic Loop." This language is exact and functional for the team.

External use (website, landing pages, social content, onboarding, intake forms):
→ Use the public-facing lexicon. The same operational logic runs behind it. The aesthetic wrapper is different.

**The rule:** The audience should never feel like they are sitting in a waiting room at a hospital. They should feel like they are stepping into a room that was built for them.

## The 30-Day Sandbox: Bridging Translate and Package

The original ABC Mindset moves from B (Translate) directly to C (Package), which can feel abrupt and overwhelming for visionary practitioners. The notebooklm critique identified this as a key drop-off point.

**The fix:** Insert a low-stakes sandbox phase between Translate and Package.

### Updated ABC Mindset: A → B → B.5 → C

| Stage | Name | What Happens |
|-------|------|-------------|
| A | Extract | Uncover the worldview — beliefs over biographies |
| B | Translate | Build the shared vocabulary — make the invisible water visible |
| **B.5** | **Sandbox / 30-Day Hypothesis** | **Test the translated vocabulary with low-stakes human evidence before committing to a build** |
| C | Package | Commercialize — the Four Ones, the First Doorway |

### What the Sandbox Phase Looks Like

The 30-Day Hypothesis is not a permission slip to play indefinitely. It is a bounded experiment with a specific evidence checkpoint.

The practitioner picks one translated offer statement and:
1. Says it to 5 real people (not AI, not in their head)
2. Observes the reaction — do they lean in or look confused?
3. Adjusts the language based on the conversation
4. Documents the feedback in the evidence log (machine-readable)
5. At day 30: does the evidence support moving to Package (C), or does it suggest more translation work?

**Evidence checkpoint at day 30:**
- [ ] Offer statement was shared with at least 5 real humans
- [ ] At least 2 genuine "that's exactly what I need" responses documented
- [ ] Language adjusted at least once based on reaction
- [ ] Clear yes/no decision on proceeding to Package

**The anti-sand trap rule:** If at day 30 there is no evidence — no conversations, no reactions, no documentation — the sandbox has become avoidance. Route to Trip Wire Detector. This is a clarity block, not a sandbox issue.

## Application: The Bali Table Language

When running a Bali Table, the external vocabulary applies throughout.

| Old | New |
|-----|-----|
| "Tell me about your bleed" | "Tell me about the thing that keeps coming back unsolved" |
| "We'll run a diagnostic" | "We'll do an ecosystem scan together" |
| "Find My Bleed" button | "What's holding you back?" or "Find my growth leak" |
| "Intervention" | "The next right thing" |
| "Prescribe a sprint" | "Here's what I'd recommend as a first step" |

The Bali Table invitation remains:
- Something worth sharing (reveals personality)
- Something worth solving (one specific, persistent problem)

The language of the table stays conversational, curious, and relational. The machine-readable evidence layer runs behind the curtain.
